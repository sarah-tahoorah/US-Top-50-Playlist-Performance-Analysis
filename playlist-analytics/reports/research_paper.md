# United States Top 50 Playlist Performance and Song Popularity Trend Analysis

**Prepared for:** Atlantic Recording Corporation — Commercial Analytics
**Scope:** Historical analysis of daily Top-50 playlist snapshots
**Observation window:** 2024-01-01 to 2024-04-29 (120 consecutive daily snapshots)
**Data:** 6,015 raw snapshot rows → 6,000 clean rows; 118 unique songs; 19 unique lead artists
**Nature of study:** Descriptive/diagnostic analytics only. No predictive or recommender models were trained.

> **Data notice.** The dataset analysed here is *synthetic sample data* generated by
> `src/generate_data.py` to mirror the schema and statistical behaviour of real Atlantic
> playlist snapshots. All figures below are reproducible from the shipped CSV, but the
> artists, titles and scores are fictional. The methodology transfers unchanged to
> production snapshot exports.

---

## 1. Introduction and business question

Playlist placement is one of the highest-leverage distribution levers available to a
label, yet placement value is not uniform: a track that spends a single week at #4 and
one that spends four months at #22 generate very different lifetime returns. The label
asked three operational questions:

1. **Artist promotion** — which artists and which songs justify additional promotional weight?
2. **Release timing** — when in a track's chart life does momentum peak, and how quickly does decay set in?
3. **Marketing spend** — which content attributes (explicit flag, single vs. album, duration, album size) correlate with chart longevity, so that spend can be allocated to the profiles that hold their placement?

This paper answers each from the historical record only.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Ingestion and validation (`src/data_pipeline.py`)

Every snapshot row is validated against the schema before any analysis runs:

| Check | Rule | Result on this dataset | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Required columns | All 10 schema columns present | 0 missing | — |
| Chart position | Integer within 1–50 | 0 violations | Offending rows dropped |
| Popularity | Numeric within 0–100 | 36 missing cells | Imputed with the per-song median, then clipped |
| Dates | Parseable to a calendar date | 0 unparseable | Offending rows dropped |
| Duplicates | Unique on (song, artist, date) | 15 duplicate keys | Best (lowest) position retained |
| Artist formatting | Trimmed, collapsed spacing, canonical `feat.` separator, consistent casing | 36 rows corrected | Normalised in place |

Artist standardisation is deliberately conservative: only strings that arrive fully
upper-case or fully lower-case are re-cased, so authored mixed-case names such as
"The Lantern Club" survive intact. Every `featuring` / `ft.` / `with` variant is folded
into a single ` feat. ` separator, and a derived `primary_artist` column (the billed-first
credit) is used for all artist-level aggregation so that a feature credit does not
fragment an artist's chart record.

### 2.2 Feature engineering (`src/features.py`)

| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| Days on Chart | Count of distinct snapshot dates on which the song appears |
| Average Rank | Mean of daily `position` |
| Best Rank Achieved | Minimum `position` observed |
| Rank Volatility Index | Sample standard deviation of daily `position` (0 = perfectly stable) |
| Popularity Trend Score | 7-day rolling mean of `popularity`; the song-level score is the final value of that series |
| Duration (min) | `duration_ms / 60000` |
| Net rank gain / Rank velocity | (first position − last position); divided by days on chart to give positions gained per charting day |
| Artist Dominance Index | `100 × (0.50 × norm(chart-days) + 0.30 × norm(unique songs) + 0.20 × clip((50 − avg rank)/49))` where `norm` is min-max scaling across artists |

The Dominance Index weighting is intentional: sustained *presence* (chart-days) is the
dominant term because it is the quantity that converts to streams, catalogue breadth is
second because it indicates a repeatable pipeline rather than one lucky record, and rank
quality is a smaller modifier that separates artists with equal presence.

### 2.3 Analysis modules (`src/analysis.py`)

Five modules, each mirrored by a dashboard tab: playlist ranking analysis, song-level
performance, artist performance, popularity score analytics, and content attribute
analysis. Each returns tidy DataFrames so the same functions serve both the paper and
the Streamlit app — there is a single source of analytical truth.

## 3. Exploratory data analysis

The chart is fully saturated: all 120 snapshots carry exactly 50 slots, filled by 18–19
lead artists on any given day. Mean daily churn is **2.5 new entries per day** (5% slot
turnover), so roughly 95% of the chart on any day was also on it yesterday. The Top 50 in
this market is a slow-moving asset, not a daily lottery.

Chart-wide average popularity oscillates in a narrow band (87.9 at the start of the
window, 87.2 at the end, trough around 86.8 in late April), i.e. total listener
engagement with the playlist is essentially flat. Competition is therefore
**zero-sum**: an artist's gain is another artist's displacement, which raises the value
of defending an existing placement relative to chasing a new entry.

Median longevity is **47 days** on chart, but the distribution is heavily right-skewed:
four songs survive the entire 120-day window, while the bottom quartile exits inside
three weeks.

## 4. Findings by analysis module

### 4.1 Playlist ranking analysis

- **Rank movement is symmetric day-to-day but asymmetric over a lifecycle.** Songs climb
  in short, steep bursts and decay in long, shallow slides. The fastest riser in the
  window ("Quiet Summers", Lorna Sky) gained **2.25 positions per charting day**, whereas
  the steepest decliner ("Faded Bones", Kira Lindqvist) shed 3.43 positions/day but did so
  from a much shorter run — decline is concentrated at end-of-life.
- **Entry/exit behaviour.** Entry positions cluster in the 30–50 band; almost nothing
  debuts inside the Top 10. The path to the Top 10 is a climb, not a debut, and that climb
  takes place in the first two weeks of chart life.
- **Popularity falls monotonically with position.** Average popularity by slot declines
  smoothly from ~100 at #1 to the low 70s at #50, with no plateau — there is no "safe
  band" where a rank slip is commercially free.

### 4.2 Song-level performance

- The longest-charting titles are also the highest-scoring: "Ivory Letters" (Saint Avery)
  held all 120 days at an average rank of 14.2 and average popularity 95.7; "Paper Cities"
  (Velvet Static) matched the 120-day run at average rank 18.7.
- **Peak rank and longevity are correlated but not interchangeable.** Splitting the
  catalogue on the median of each axis produces four segments:

  | Segment | Songs | Interpretation |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hit & Stay | 41 | Strong peak *and* long run — flagship catalogue assets |
  | Marginal | 40 | Weak peak, short run — minimal ROI on further spend |
  | Flash Hit | 20 | Strong peak, short run — spike-driven; candidates for a re-push |
  | Slow Burner | 20 | Modest peak, long run — quietly efficient stream earners |

  The 20 Flash Hits are the most actionable group: they have already demonstrated they can
  reach a high position, and their short run is a sustain problem, not a demand problem.

### 4.3 Artist performance

| Artist | Chart-days | Unique songs | Avg rank | Top-10 days | Dominance Index |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dust & Ember | 749 | 12 | 24.3 | 173 | **90.5** |
| Saint Avery | 658 | 11 | 20.2 | 206 | 83.1 |
| Bea Whitlock | 471 | 9 | 25.6 | 84 | 62.3 |
| Milo & The Tides | 384 | 9 | 31.9 | 45 | 54.0 |
| Jaxon Frey | 416 | 6 | 25.7 | 107 | 49.6 |

Two distinct dominance strategies are visible. **Dust & Ember** dominates by *breadth* —
12 charting titles producing 749 song-days at a middling average rank. **Saint Avery**
dominates by *quality* — fewer chart-days but 206 Top-10 days and the best average rank in
the set. Weekly slot-share data shows the leading pair jointly holding 20–25% of the chart
throughout the window with no erosion, so dominance here is structural rather than a
single-release artefact.

### 4.4 Popularity score analytics

- **Popularity and rank are near-deterministically linked**: Pearson r = **−0.947**,
  Spearman ρ = **−0.953** over 6,000 observations. Popularity is effectively a restatement
  of rank, not an independent signal, so it should not be used as a second, "confirming"
  KPI in the same decision.

  | Rank band | n | Mean popularity | Std dev | Min |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Top 10 | 1,200 | 99.7 | 1.1 | 92 |
  | Top 11–20 | 1,200 | 95.5 | 3.5 | 80 |
  | Top 21–50 | 3,600 | 81.5 | 6.8 | 63 |

- **Variance rises as rank falls.** The Top 10 is nearly variance-free (σ = 1.1); the
  21–50 band carries six times the dispersion. The lower half of the chart is where
  outcomes are genuinely uncertain, and therefore where promotional intervention has the
  largest possible effect.
- **Rank volatility correlates positively with longevity** (r = 0.40 between the Rank
  Volatility Index and days on chart). Long-lived songs necessarily traverse more of the
  chart; a high volatility reading on a long-running title is normal, whereas high
  volatility on a *short* run is the genuine instability signal.

### 4.5 Content attribute analysis

| Comparison | Group | Songs | Avg days on chart | Avg best rank | Avg popularity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit flag | Explicit | 55 | **52.3** | 15.8 | 86.6 |
| | Clean | 66 | 47.3 | 18.0 | 84.5 |
| Album type | Album track | 62 | **53.8** | 15.2 | 86.8 |
| | Single | 59 | 45.2 | 18.9 | 84.1 |

- **Explicit tracks outperform on every measure** — five extra days on chart, a two-position
  better peak, and two points of popularity. The explicit share of the chart also *grew*
  across the window, from 32% of slots on day one to **48%** at the end. This is a trend,
  not a constant, and any content policy that suppresses explicit releases is fighting the
  market direction.
- **Album tracks outlast singles** by 8.6 days on average and peak three positions higher.
  Album context appears to supply a discovery tail that standalone singles lack.
- **Duration effects are weak and non-monotonic.** Sub-2:30 tracks show the best average
  rank (26.6) and joint-longest runs (57.6 days), while the crowded 3:00–3:30 band performs
  worst on longevity (43.8 days). The effect size is modest and duration should be treated
  as a tiebreaker, not a selection criterion.
- **Album size shows a longevity effect at the extremes.** Tracks from 13–16-track albums
  averaged **61.2** chart-days and 17+-track albums 57.4, against 46.8 for 9–12-track
  albums. Larger projects sustain placement longer, consistent with a broader
  cross-promotion surface.

## 5. Recommendations

**Artist promotion**

1. **Defend, don't just acquire.** With 5% daily slot churn and flat aggregate popularity,
   holding an existing placement is cheaper than winning a new one. Set a standing
   "placement defence" budget for every title inside the Top 20.
2. **Re-push the 20 Flash Hits.** These titles proved they can reach a strong peak but did
   not sustain. They are the highest-expected-value targets for a second promotional wave,
   ahead of unproven new releases.
3. **Treat the two dominance archetypes differently.** Breadth artists (Dust & Ember
   profile) reward release *frequency*; quality artists (Saint Avery profile) reward
   concentrated spend behind fewer, stronger records aimed at Top-10 days.

**Release timing**

4. **Front-load spend into the first 14 days.** Nothing debuts in the Top 10 — position is
   won during an early climb. Spend deployed after a title's peak has passed is chasing a
   decay curve.
5. **Sequence releases against a saturated chart.** Because the playlist is zero-sum and
   the leading two artists hold 20–25% of slots continuously, avoid launching two owned
   titles into the same week; they will cannibalise each other's climb window.

**Marketing spend**

6. **Weight spend toward album-context and explicit-flagged releases.** Both attributes
   independently associate with longer runs and better peaks, and explicit share of the
   chart is trending upward (32% → 48%).
7. **Target the 21–50 band for intervention.** It carries six times the popularity variance
   of the Top 10, which is where marketing input can still change the outcome; spend on
   Top-10 titles largely subsidises an already-determined result.
8. **Retire "Marginal" titles quickly.** 40 songs sit in the weak-peak/short-run quadrant.
   A hard stop-loss on further promotional spend here releases budget for the Flash Hit
   re-push programme.

---

## 6. Limitations

- The dataset is synthetic; effect *sizes* should be re-estimated on production snapshots
  before budget is committed, even though the methodology and KPI definitions carry over.
- The analysis is observational. Attribute associations (explicit, album type, duration)
  are correlational and confounded by promotional spend, which is not present in the
  snapshot schema.
- Only chart-facing metrics are available. Streams, saves, skip rate and playlist-follower
  counts would materially sharpen the ROI arguments in Section 5.
- Popularity is supplied as a derived Atlantic API score whose internal construction is
  opaque; its near-perfect correlation with rank means it adds little independent
  information in this dataset.
